## G1. A business that is deactivated

The platform team can deactivate a business (for example one breaking the rules) and later reactivate it. While it is
deactivated, nobody in it can sign in. Suppose it is deactivated while it owes one or more months.

- ☐ **What it owes stays owed, but nothing is taken while it is deactivated. It isn't charged for the time it was
  deactivated. Once reactivated, it is billed again from its next billing date, and what it owed is taken as usual**
  *(Our suggestion: like switching billing off (F4): debts are kept, and nobody gets a backdated bill.)*
- ☐ What it owes is cancelled (kept in its records as "waived"), as for a business marked "not billed" (F1)

## G2. A payment that fails because of TransactPay, not the business

Sometimes a charge fails for a reason that isn't the business's: TransactPay is down, or something is wrong in
AlphaKolect's setup. After a few tries, that month's charge is put on hold until the problem is fixed. A month on hold
never suspends a business by itself.

Now suppose a business is suspended because its wallet was short, then loads its wallet with enough for everything it
owes. Every month is taken, except the one on hold, which still can't be taken because of the problem on TransactPay's
or AlphaKolect's side. You chose that access returns only when everything owed is paid (Q5).

- ☐ **Access returns once everything except the month on hold is paid. The month on hold stays owed and is taken as
  soon as the problem is fixed, without suspending the business again for it** *(Our suggestion: the business has done
  all it can, and the failure isn't its fault.)*
- ☐ Access returns only when the month on hold is paid too, as Q5 says
